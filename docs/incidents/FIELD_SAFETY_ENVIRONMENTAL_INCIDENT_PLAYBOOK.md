# WashOps field safety and environmental incident playbook

**Purpose:** protect people, stop additional harm, preserve facts, and route
required human decisions after a field incident  
**Incident commander:** company owner until explicitly handed off  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-30  
**Status:** operational control; not medical, legal, environmental, insurance,
or regulatory advice

Print or cache this playbook and current product SDSs before field work. Do not
depend on connectivity, the affected equipment, or an AI agent during an
emergency. Emergency responders, product labels/SDSs, qualified professionals,
and authorities control.

## Universal first five minutes

1. **Protect people.** Stop work. Move people out of danger without creating a
   second exposure or fall. Contact local emergency services for an immediate
   threat or serious injury. Do not delay care to finish this form.
2. **Make the scene safer only within training.** Shut down, isolate,
   depressurize, disconnect, contain, or evacuate only when the trained human can
   do so safely and consistently with the equipment manual, label/SDS, and SOP.
3. **Prevent additional exposure.** Establish an exclusion zone. Keep customers,
   children, pets, traffic, and unprotected workers away. Do not wash an unknown
   chemical into a drain or improvise a neutralizer.
4. **Set one incident commander and recorder.** Record exact local time/timezone,
   address, people, service, task, equipment, product, observed conditions, and
   action taken. Use facts; do not diagnose or assign blame.
5. **Preserve evidence.** Keep original photos/video, product containers/labels/
   SDS revisions, equipment, failed parts, weather observations, messages,
   checklist, time/material records, provider/audit IDs, and witness contacts.
   Do not alter, discard, repair, or reenact unless needed to protect life or
   directed by an authority.

AI may organize supplied facts into an internal timeline. It may not give
treatment or cleanup instructions, decide reportability, contact anyone
externally, draft a customer admission/release, delete evidence, or authorize
resumption.

## Severity

| Level     | Examples                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Required response                                                                                   |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `FIELD-1` | Fatality, life-threatening injury/exposure, fall from height, uncontrolled chemical release/runoff/backflow threat, fire/electrical event, public danger                                                      | Emergency response; stop all related operations; owner leads safety/legal/insurer/regulatory review |
| `FIELD-2` | Medical evaluation, suspected pressure injection, chemical eye/skin/inhalation exposure, contained spill with uncertain reporting, property damage, vehicle event, lost containment, public/customer exposure | Stop affected work; prompt qualified evaluation; owner opens incident and notification review       |
| `FIELD-3` | Near miss, controlled equipment defect, minor contained release with no exposure, stop-work deviation caught before harm                                                                                      | Keep work stopped until correction and supervisor review; record near miss                          |
| `FIELD-4` | Checklist/data issue with no physical or environmental exposure                                                                                                                                               | Correct through normal controlled workflow; escalate if evidence was hidden or fabricated           |

When uncertain, use the higher level until a qualified human reviews it.

## Pressure spray or injection injury

- Stop and depressurize the system only when safe.
- Treat a high-pressure spray/injection wound as potentially serious even if the
  opening appears small. Arrange prompt professional medical evaluation under
  the emergency plan.
- Send the exact product label/SDS, pressure-washer model, nozzle/attachment,
  pressure setting if known, and injection material with the injured person or
  responder.
- Quarantine the machine, hose, fittings, gun, nozzle, and failed component.
  Preserve settings and photos; do not test-fire or repair before release.
- Use the current official
  [CDC Pressure Washer Safety](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/pressure-washer-safety.html)
  page as background, not individualized medical advice.

## Ladder, roof, window, or fall event

- Do not move a fallen person unless required to escape immediate danger or
  directed by emergency professionals.
- Stop all work at height and secure the drop zone. Do not send another person
  onto a roof/ladder/rope system without the reviewed rescue capability.
- Preserve ladder/fall equipment, anchor documentation, access method, roof/
  window condition, footing, barricades, weather, training, inspection, and
  rescue-plan evidence.
- A rope-descent or fall-arrest load requires qualified equipment/anchorage
  disposition before reuse. OSHA’s current rope-descent requirements include
  anchorage documentation, equipment inspection, independent fall arrest, and
  prompt rescue:
  [29 CFR 1910.27](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.27).

## Chemical exposure, spill, or fire

- Identify the exact product from its original label/container; do not rely on
  color, smell, a handwritten nickname, or AI recognition.
- Follow emergency responders and the current label/SDS for first aid, fire,
  incompatibility, PPE, isolation, containment, and disposal. Never mix a second
  product to “cancel” the first unless the approved emergency plan expressly
  directs it.
- Preserve product/lot, estimated quantity and concentration only when known,
  release time/duration, media affected, injuries, weather, containment, waste,
  and responders.
- If the product or quantity is unknown, say unknown. Do not reverse-engineer a
  value from purchase history.
- TCEQ explains that reporting depends on the substance, quantity, and location
  and provides the official state path:
  [TCEQ Spills, Discharges, and Releases](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/response/spills).
  The incident commander routes the facts immediately to the qualified
  environmental/legal reviewer; AI does not calculate a reportable quantity.

## Wash-water escape, storm drain, or off-property discharge

- Stop the water/chemical source and work upstream. Protect people and traffic.
- Deploy only the preplanned containment/recovery method within training; do not
  enter a drain, open a manhole, or redirect contaminated water to another
  unapproved location.
- Record the source, products/pollutants, approximate quantity only if supportable,
  start/stop time, path, storm/sanitary/drain identity, property boundary,
  waterway/soil/vegetation affected or threatened, weather, and containment/
  recovery.
- Notify the owner and the municipality/environmental reviewer under the approved
  plan. Fort Worth and Dallas have different official requirements:
  [Fort Worth mobile commercial cosmetic cleaning](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/environmental-services/environmental-quality/stormwater-quality/powerwash)
  and
  [Dallas pavement-washing tips](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/waterutilities/stormwater-operations/PublishingImages/Keep%20Stormwater%20Clean%20Feb%2023_2021.pdf).
- Do not resume that service or disposal path until written human authorization
  and corrective controls are attached.

## Suspected backflow or potable-water contamination

- Stop the connection and affected water use when safe. Prevent people from
  drinking or using potentially affected water until the responsible water
  authority/qualified professional directs otherwise.
- Preserve the hose/injector/tank/device arrangement, exact products, time,
  pressure-loss or other trigger, property fixtures affected, and backflow
  inspection/test records.
- Notify the owner and water provider through the reviewed emergency plan.
  TCEQ explains that hose-end soap attachments and hoses placed into gutters/
  downspouts can create cross-connections:
  [TCEQ Cross-Connection Control and Backflow Prevention](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/drinkingwater/cross-connection).
- AI and the technician may not declare the water safe, select a corrective
  device, or authorize flushing/testing.

## Heat, lightning, wind, or severe-weather event

- Stop work and move to the reviewed shelter/cooling/emergency response.
- For heat symptoms, use the approved heat emergency plan and prompt medical
  response. Do not ask the affected person to “push through” or let AI decide
  fitness to resume.
- When thunder is heard or lightning threatens, move to a substantial building or
  hard-topped vehicle; NWS says no outdoor location is safe during a thunderstorm:
  [NWS Lightning Safety](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-safety).
- Preserve NWS alert/forecast issue time, actual site observation, workload,
  acclimatization/break/fluid records where applicable, PPE, supervisor decisions,
  and the exact stop/shelter/resumption timeline.
- Suspend related roof, ladder, pole, chemical-spray, and trailer operations until
  the owner applies the reviewed SOP to current conditions.

## Property damage, public/customer exposure, or vehicle/equipment event

- Protect people, utilities, traffic, and the property from additional harm.
- Do not promise coverage, accept liability, authorize repair, refund, or sign a
  release at the scene.
- Record pre-work condition evidence, exact work underway, equipment/product,
  customer/public statements verbatim when practical, witnesses, photos, and
  protective actions.
- Notify the owner and insurer under the policy. Customer, legal, safety, refund,
  and public-review wording requires human approval.
- For a vehicle/trailer event, also preserve driver, vehicle/trailer/load,
  registration, inspection, route, damage, law-enforcement/insurer identifiers,
  and any chemical/equipment release.

## Human notification and deadline review

The incident commander starts this review immediately; do not wait for root
cause. Record who decided applicability and the source/version used.

| Potential duty                                   | Human reviewer                      | Evidence/decision |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Emergency services / medical response            | Incident commander / responders     |                   |
| Customer/property owner                          | Owner + counsel/insurer             |                   |
| Insurer / workers’ compensation carrier          | Owner + licensed agent/counsel      |                   |
| OSHA fatality/severe-injury report               | Employment/safety counsel           |                   |
| Texas DWC subscriber/non-subscriber report       | Texas employment/insurance reviewer |                   |
| TCEQ spill/release report and follow-up          | Environmental counsel/professional  |                   |
| Municipality, sewer authority, or water provider | Environmental/legal reviewer        |                   |
| Product/equipment manufacturer or vendor         | Safety/legal/insurer reviewer       |                   |
| Privacy/data-breach notification                 | Privacy/security counsel            |                   |

OSHA states that covered employers must report an employee fatality within
eight hours and an in-patient hospitalization, amputation, or eye loss within
24 hours:
[OSHA Report a Fatality or Severe Injury](https://www.osha.gov/report/).
Partial routine-recordkeeping exemptions do not by themselves remove severe-
event reporting. Counsel must determine employer/employee, work-relatedness,
event, timing, and reporting applicability; WashOps may preserve a deadline but
must not decide or submit it.

Texas DWC says private employers generally may choose workers’ compensation,
while subscribers and non-subscribers have different notice/reporting duties:
[Texas Department of Insurance — Employer Resources](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employer/).
Do not infer a duty or exemption from the company’s headcount alone.

TCEQ requires prompt review of releases against current reportable-quantity and
location rules and may require follow-up:
[TCEQ Spill Rules and Laws](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/response/spills/spill_rules.html).
Notification to one authority may not satisfy another.

## Evidence packet

Use [WashOps Incident Report](../templates/INCIDENT_REPORT.md) and attach:

- exact location/jurisdiction, service, scope, task, people, witnesses, and
  responder/authority/claim identifiers;
- original timeline with timezones and how each time was obtained;
- injury/exposure facts without diagnosis;
- exact products, labels, SDS revisions, quantities known/unknown, equipment,
  serials, inspections, settings, and failed parts;
- weather/alerts and observed site conditions;
- water source/backflow and drain/runoff/disposal evidence;
- before/during/after photos and customer/public communications;
- checklist, time, material, signature, approval, AI trace, webhook,
  idempotency, offline queue, and audit IDs when relevant;
- every notification decision, reviewer, deadline, submission/claim ID, and
  follow-up; and
- containment, cleanup/disposal receipts, corrective action, training, and
  recovery approval.

Keep medical, personnel, insurance, legal, and customer records access-restricted.
Do not place unnecessary sensitive details in AI prompts or broad audit notes.

## Recovery gate

- [ ] People are safe and immediate environmental/property hazards are controlled.
- [ ] Required responders, owner, reviewers, insurer, and authorities were
      contacted or a qualified human documented why not.
- [ ] Original evidence and affected equipment/product are preserved.
- [ ] Customer, worker, public, property, water, and environmental impact is known
      or explicitly unknown.
- [ ] Root cause and contributing conditions are reviewed without rewriting the
      historical record.
- [ ] Corrective action, training, equipment disposition, cleanup/disposal, and
      follow-up have owners/dates.
- [ ] The affected service, product, equipment, crew, site class, automation, and
      disposal path remain disabled until separately authorized.
- [ ] A relevant drill/test proves the revised control.
- [ ] Safety, legal, environmental, insurer, and owner recovery approvals are
      attached.

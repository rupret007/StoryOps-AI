# Exterior-cleaning safety and environmental checklist

**Applies to:** pressure washing, soft washing, gutter cleaning, and downspout cleaning  
**Verified against official sources:** July 28, 2026  
**Use:** pre-service SOP approval plus a fresh copy for every visit

> **REQUIRED SAFETY, ENVIRONMENTAL, CHEMICAL, AND LEGAL REVIEW:** This checklist
> is a control framework, not a chemical recipe, disposal authorization,
> equipment manual, rescue plan, or substitute for a competent safety
> professional. The owner must adopt site- and product-specific SOPs after
> review. Manufacturer manuals, current product labels/SDSs, applicable law,
> municipal direction, and trained human judgment control. AI may identify
> missing evidence and stop work; it may never invent or approve safety or
> chemical instructions.

## Non-negotiable stop-work rules

- [ ] Stop if the operator is untrained, impaired, ill, fatigued, or lacks the
      approved PPE/equipment for the scope.
- [ ] Stop if the address, property authorization, scope, surface, measurements,
      weather, access, utilities, chemical, wastewater path, or emergency plan is
      unknown or conflicts with the work order.
- [ ] Stop if children, customers, pets, traffic, other trades, or the public
      cannot be kept outside the controlled work area.
- [ ] Stop for unapproved roof entry, fall exposure, unstable/incorrect ladder
      setup, overhead electrical hazards, unsafe wind/weather, damaged equipment,
      leaks, injection injury, uncontrolled overspray, chemical exposure, spill, or
      wash water approaching a prohibited drain/property boundary.
- [ ] A stop-work event cannot be overridden by an AI agent. Resumption requires
      an authorized human, documented corrective action, and any required
      safety/environmental approval.

## Before dispatch

- [ ] Human-verify the service address, jurisdiction, route, access, service
      scope, price-book version, customer authorization, and whether anyone will be
      present.
- [ ] Check current forecast, watches/warnings, precipitation, temperature,
      lightning, and wind using the approved weather source. Weather data is
      evidence, not permission to work. An owner-approved SOP must define thresholds
      by task/equipment; uncertainty blocks booking.
- [ ] Confirm the exact equipment and current manufacturer manual. Check
      inspection/maintenance status and remove/tag defective equipment from service.
- [ ] Confirm every product by exact manufacturer, product name, concentration/
      formulation, label revision, SDS revision, approved use/site, container label,
      and secured transport. Do not substitute products.
- [ ] Confirm the job-specific wash-water containment and lawful disposal plan.
      “Water only,” “biodegradable,” customer permission, or a nearby drain is not a
      generic discharge authorization.
- [ ] Confirm an emergency communication method, exact address/location,
      customer/site contact, nearest safe access point for responders, SDS access,
      first-aid supplies, eyewash/washing capability specified by the reviewed SOP,
      and incident escalation path.

## Site arrival and hazard assessment

- [ ] Walk the work area before unloading. Record surfaces, slopes, drains,
      downspout outlets, pools/water features, landscaping, vehicles, fragile/
      oxidized coatings, loose material, open windows/doors, electrical sources,
      overhead lines, trip hazards, traffic, pets, and adjacent property.
- [ ] Photograph pre-existing conditions and get human confirmation for changed
      or uncertain scope. Photos are evidence; they do not prove material,
      structural integrity, dimensions, chemical compatibility, or safety.
- [ ] Establish exclusion zones, signs/cones/barriers, hose management, spotter
      requirements, and safe equipment placement. Do not leave a running machine or
      active chemical unattended.
- [ ] Complete the task-specific PPE hazard assessment. OSHA states employers
      must assess likely hazards, select suitable PPE, communicate the selection,
      and document the assessment:
      [OSHA PPE assessment](https://www.osha.gov/training/library/personal-protective-equipment/assessment).
      **REQUIRED SAFETY/LEGAL REVIEW:** a competent reviewer must select PPE from
      the actual hazards, equipment manual, product label, and SDS; this application
      must not recommend PPE by guessing from a photo or service name.

## Pressure equipment and power

- [ ] Follow the current owner’s manual, guards, trigger/spray controls,
      grounding/electrical protection, hose/fitting ratings, start/shutdown,
      depressurization, maintenance, and safe-distance requirements.
- [ ] Never point the spray at a person or use it to move an object. Keep people
      out of the spray/debris path. CDC warns that high-pressure spray can cause
      serious wounds that initially appear minor:
      [CDC Pressure Washer Safety](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/pressure-washer-safety.html).
- [ ] Treat any high-pressure injection/spray wound as urgent. Stop, initiate the
      reviewed emergency plan, provide responders the product/SDS and equipment
      information, and obtain prompt professional medical evaluation. AI must not
      diagnose, downgrade, or improvise treatment.
- [ ] Never operate gasoline-powered equipment in an enclosed or semi-enclosed
      space or near an opening contrary to the manufacturer/approved plan. CDC/NIOSH
      documents fatal carbon-monoxide incidents involving gasoline pressure washers:
      [NIOSH carbon-monoxide alert](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/96-118a/).
- [ ] Keep water, cords, connectors, receptacles, service entrances, fixtures,
      and energized equipment within the reviewed electrical-safety plan. Stop if
      conditions differ from the plan.

## Ladders, gutters, and work at height

- [ ] Use ground-access methods when the approved scope and equipment allow.
      Roof entry is prohibited unless a separate reviewed roof/fall-protection SOP,
      training, insurance approval, equipment, rescue plan, and job authorization
      are all present.
- [ ] Inspect the ladder before use; remove and tag damaged ladders. Verify type,
      duty rating, length, feet, locks, level/stable footing, angle, top support,
      securing/barricading, door/traffic control, and clearance from electricity.
- [ ] Face the ladder and maintain three-point contact while climbing. Do not
      carry a load that defeats safe climbing. OSHA’s current summary is the
      [Portable Ladder Safety QuickCard](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/portable_ladder_qc.pdf);
      applicable standards and the task-specific plan control.
- [ ] Stop if reaching, hose force, wet/slippery rungs, wind, uneven soil,
      obstacles, customer traffic, or the need to work outside the ladder’s design
      creates an uncontrolled hazard.
- [ ] Before a downspout test/flush, verify outlet location, downstream condition,
      customer authorization, and the approved discharge/containment path. Do not
      assume an underground connection’s destination.

## Chemicals, labels, and SDS

- [ ] Maintain a current SDS and original/manufacturer label for every product
      where immediately accessible during the work. OSHA requires labels, SDS
      access, a written hazard-communication program, and training where the Hazard
      Communication Standard applies:
      [OSHA Hazard Communication](https://www.osha.gov/hazcom/) and
      [29 CFR 1910.1200](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200).
- [ ] Use only an owner-approved product for the exact labeled use/site and
      approved application method. Follow its current label and SDS for handling,
      dilution (if any), incompatibilities, PPE, ventilation, storage, spill,
      first-aid, disposal, and environmental precautions.
- [ ] Never let AI create a mixture, ratio, compatibility decision, substitution,
      application method, exposure response, or disposal instruction. If the label/
      SDS is absent, illegible, conflicting, or outside the approved SOP, stop.
- [ ] Do not mix bleach/disinfectant with other cleaners. CDC warns that doing so
      can release dangerous vapors:
      [CDC Cleaning and Disinfecting with Bleach](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/cleaning-and-disinfecting-with-bleach.html).
      This warning is not a formulation guide.
- [ ] **REQUIRED CHEMICAL/PESTICIDE LEGAL REVIEW.** Review products, website,
      quotes, scripts, and technician language for claims about killing, preventing,
      controlling, or mitigating algae, mold, mildew, bacteria, or other pests.
      EPA explains that claims can cause a cleaning product to be regulated as a
      pesticide, including claims made in advertising:
      [EPA cleaning-product/FIFRA guidance](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/determining-if-cleaning-product-pesticide-under-fifra).
      If a regulated product is used, its legally enforceable label controls:
      [EPA Introduction to Pesticide Labels](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-labels/introduction-pesticide-labels).
      Counsel and the relevant regulator must determine registration, applicator,
      recordkeeping, and advertising requirements.

## Heat and weather

- [ ] Use the owner-approved heat program: acclimatization, potable fluids,
      electrolyte availability where applicable, shade/cooling, scheduled recovery,
      workload adjustment, monitoring, communication, symptoms, and emergency
      response. OSHA’s current prevention guidance emphasizes water, rest, and
      shade:
      [OSHA Heat—Water, Rest, Shade](https://www.osha.gov/heat-exposure/water-rest-shade).
- [ ] Stop and follow the emergency plan when a worker reports or displays a
      heat-illness warning sign. AI does not determine fitness to resume.
- [ ] Stop for lightning, unsafe wind, flooding, severe weather, freezing/slip
      conditions, low visibility, or any condition outside the task SOP. Never infer
      safe roof/ladder operation from a general daily forecast.

## Wash water and environmental controls

- [ ] Map every storm inlet, curb flow path, drainage feature, sanitary access
      point, landscaping route, property boundary, and receiving area before work.
      Verify plumbing destination; an outdoor drain is not presumed sanitary.
- [ ] Pre-clean and contain removed debris/contaminants using the approved SOP.
      Establish containment before water/chemical starts and continuously monitor
      it. Stop before overflow or off-property movement.
- [ ] **REQUIRED ENVIRONMENTAL/LEGAL REVIEW.** Obtain municipality/property/
      sewer-authority authorization for the exact disposal path. Stormwater can
      carry pollutants to water without treatment, as EPA explains:
      [EPA Sources and Solutions—Stormwater](https://www.epa.gov/nutrientpollution/sources-and-solutions-stormwater).
- [ ] **REQUIRED FORT WORTH LEGAL/ENVIRONMENTAL REVIEW.** When the site is in
      Fort Worth, verify the current mobile-cleaner permit/unit registration and
      follow the city-approved job-specific discharge plan:
      [Fort Worth power-washer regulation](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/environmental-services/environmental-quality/stormwater-quality/powerwash).
- [ ] **REQUIRED DALLAS LEGAL/ENVIRONMENTAL REVIEW.** When the site is in Dallas,
      verify the current plan against
      [Dallas pavement-washing guidance](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/waterutilities/stormwater-operations/PublishingImages/Keep%20Stormwater%20Clean%20Feb%2023_2021.pdf)
      and written municipal direction.
- [ ] Store disposal authorization, source, date/version, waste/water type,
      containment method, destination, quantity if required, receipts/manifests, and
      before/during/after evidence. Never fabricate a disposal record.

## Closeout

- [ ] Shut down, isolate, depressurize, clean, inspect, secure, and load equipment
      per manufacturer/SOP. Account for products, waste, debris, tools, and barriers.
- [ ] Verify drains/property boundaries are clear of prohibited discharge and
      that no slip, trip, overspray, open-access, chemical, equipment, or property
      hazard remains.
- [ ] Capture after photos, measurements actually observed, materials actually
      used, start/stop times, checklist answers, disposal evidence, exceptions,
      customer notes, and signature. Never backfill evidence from estimates.
- [ ] Create an incident/near-miss record for injury, exposure, spill, discharge,
      equipment defect, property damage, complaint, stop-work, or material
      deviation. Preserve original photos/messages/logs and notify the reviewed
      escalation chain. Legal/safety/customer messages require approval.
- [ ] A customer signature acknowledges the documented handoff only; it does not
      waive incident reporting, legal duties, or internal review.
